# Testing the website with farmers

The brand and mobile proposal (25 October 2026) asks Oceania to validate before expanding: test with 6 to 8 farmers, check ordinary phones and slow connections, and measure the conversations the site starts. This guide is for the Oceania team members who will run those sessions. It needs no technical knowledge.

Run it once before launch, or in the first weeks after. Run it again after any large change.

## 1. Who to invite

- 6 to 8 farmers who could really buy from Oceania, not staff or their relatives.
- A real mix:
  - younger and older farmers, women and men
  - different crops and farm sizes
  - people who are comfortable with English and people who are not

  The proposal warns against designing for a single invented "typical farmer".
- Start where Oceania already works (Maharashtra), but include someone from outside it if you can.

## 2. Before each session

- **Consent.** Explain in the farmer's language:
  - the website is being tested, not them, and they can stop at any time
  - what you will note down
  - that the session is never used as a testimonial

  Record their agreement and its date. Record the screen or voice only if they agree to that too. A farmer who later wants to appear in a farmer story or video gives that consent separately (see `cms-guide.md`).
- **Phone.** Use the farmer's own phone if they agree. Otherwise use an ordinary, inexpensive Android phone a few years old, not a new phone from the office.
- **Address.** Use the live site, or the preview address if testing before launch.
- **Team.** One person guides the session in the farmer's language; a second takes notes.
- **Staff area.** Tell whoever handles enquiries that test requests will arrive. Afterwards, delete them in the staff area (open the lead, then "Delete this lead"). If a farmer asks a real question during the test, treat it as a real enquiry.
- **During the tasks, do not help.** If the farmer asks what to do, say "What would you do if I were not here?" Note where they got stuck.

## 3. Tasks

Describe each task as a situation, without using the words on the screen. After each one, ask "How easy was that?" and "What was confusing?"

| # | Say this | It worked if the farmer... |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Show the homepage for five seconds, then take the phone back. "What is this website for? Who is it for?" | says it is about farming inputs and advice, in their own words |
| 2 | "The leaves of your crop are turning yellow. Ask Oceania for help." | reaches Get crop advice, or WhatsApp or Call, and fills in the form or knows how |
| 3 | "You want to buy one can of Halad Special. Show me how." | reaches the order form, chooses the pack and number, and can say what happens next: the team calls about price and delivery, and nothing is paid online |
| 4 | "You would like someone from Oceania to see your field." | reaches Field visits and chooses the kind of visit they want |
| 5 | "Find something that helps you learn about your soil." Once farmer videos are published: "Find a video from a farmer and start it." | reaches Learn and opens a guide, a practice or a video |
| 6 | "You want to talk to someone at Oceania right now." | uses WhatsApp or Call, for example from the bar at the bottom of the screen (once the numbers are set) |

Finally, ask: "Let's understand farming together: what does that mean to you?"

## 4. What to watch for

- Hesitation, scrolling back and forth, or tapping something that is not a link.
- Words the farmer does not understand or reads aloud slowly. Note each word.
- Holding the phone close, or zooming in. The text may be too small for them.
- Whether they notice the bar at the bottom of the screen.
- Whether they trust the order request, and understand that it is a request and not a payment.
- What they expected to find and did not.

## 5. Phones and connections

Check these yourself, on the same kind of phone:

- **Slow connection:** test where the signal is weak, such as in a field, or switch the phone to 3G. Note how long the homepage takes to show its headline and photo.
- **Large text:** set the phone's text size to large and repeat task 3. Nothing should be cut off.
- **Contact:** WhatsApp and Call open the right app with Oceania's number, and an order's WhatsApp message carries its reference.
- **Forms:** a request sent on a slow connection shows its reference number, and appears in the staff area.

## 6. The next day: what they remember

With the farmer's agreement, call them the next day. Ask:

- "What do you remember about the Oceania website?"
- "What can you do there?"
- "Would you use it? For what?"

Write down what they remember without prompting. That is what the site has really taught them.

## 7. Recording and deciding

For each farmer, note each task as done, done with help, or not done, with the time taken, comments and their exact words. Then:

1. List the five problems that came up most often.
2. Change the wording or layout. Most wording changes can be made in the editor.
3. Test the changes with two or three new farmers.

## 8. Measuring after launch

The proposal asks for qualified conversations, completed callbacks, visits and orders:

- **Requests:** the staff area shows the last 30 days of crop advice requests, order requests and field visit requests.
- **Outcomes:** callbacks completed, visits made and orders confirmed happen outside the website. Record them in the lead's status and internal notes in the staff area, or in the team's own records.
- **Taps and plays:** if Google Analytics is switched on (see `client-checklist.md`, Decisions), it also counts:
  - taps on WhatsApp, Call and Get advice (`contact_click`)
  - video plays (`video_play`)
  - stored requests (`generate_lead`)

As the proposal says, set targets and response times after the first month, once there is a baseline.
